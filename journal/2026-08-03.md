# 2026-08-03

## Batch-size experiment

- The owner immediately identified the confound in an equal-step comparison: batch 64 sees
  64× as many tokens as batch 1. Taught the equal-token invariant, the tradeoff between
  gradient variance and update count, hardware saturation, and why LR must be reconsidered
  as batch changes.
- Added independent `eval_batch_size` configuration so every run averages the same four
  batch-32 validation samples. Without this, validation noise would shrink with training
  batch and bias the curves. Official tests remain **46 passed, 2 platform skips**; Ruff is
  green.
- Capacity probes found batch 128 used about 19.0 GiB of MPS driver memory, while batch 256
  used 38.5 GiB versus PyTorch's 37.4 GiB recommended limit. Batch 256 completed but is the
  practical boundary rather than a safe operating point.
- Ran batch 1, 8, 32, 64, 128, and 256 on exactly **2,097,152 tokens**, aligning eight
  validation points by processed tokens. Initial LRs followed linear batch scaling from
  `1e-3` at batch 32; poor large-batch cases were re-run at half LR.
- Best result per batch: B1 **3.551 / 333.8 s**, B8 **3.005 / 210.8 s**, B32
  **2.907 / 173.2 s**, B64 **3.212 / 165.4 s**, B128 **3.658 / 178.6 s**, B256
  **4.349 / 206.3 s**. Effective throughput peaked at B64 (12.68k tok/s), only 4.7%
  above B32 (12.11k tok/s), at a 0.304 validation-loss cost.
- Retuning separated two effects. Lowering B128 from `4e-3` to `2e-3` improved loss from
  3.939 to 3.658; lowering B256 from `8e-3` to `4e-3` improved 5.007 to 4.349. But B64's
  lower `1e-3` LR was worse than `2e-3` (3.296 vs 3.212). LR instability explained part,
  not all, of the large-batch deficit. At equal tokens, too few updates and memory/bandwidth
  saturation dominate beyond the useful B32–B64 region.
