## TinyStories encoding and learning-rate sweep

- Implemented a resumable, boundary-parallel encoder that writes raw `uint16` token arrays.
  A 200-document fixture produced 3,724 IDs exactly equal to whole-file encoding and decoded
  back to the original text.
- Encoded the full TinyStories training set to **541,229,347 tokens** (1,082,458,694 output
  bytes) in 204.4 s, and validation to **5,465,883 tokens** in 2.0 s. File sizes, ID ranges,
  and document-delimiter counts passed sanity checks.
- Benchmarked the handout's 4-layer, 512-dimensional base model on MPS at roughly
  13k–18k tokens/s, making the 40.96M-token low-resource run feasible.
- The owner predicted the three coarse learning-rate regimes correctly: `1e-5` too slow,
  `3e-4` normal, and `3e-2` highly unstable. The controlled sweep held model, seed, data,
  optimizer, batch, token budget, evaluation batches, and schedule shape constant.
- Final validation loss after 100 steps: `1e-5` **8.5960**, `1e-4` **5.7391**, `3e-4`
  **4.2651**, `1e-3` **3.6256**, and `3e-2` **4.6333**. At `3e-2`, training loss spiked
  to 10.801 and the pre-clip gradient norm to 5.845; warmup and clipping prevented NaNs but
  did not make it competitive. `1e-3` is the short-run winner, not yet proof of the best
  5,000-step endpoint.
- After the owner questioned the source and coverage of the chosen rates, filled the omitted
  logarithmic points. `3e-3` reached **3.5892**, narrowly better than `1e-3`; `1e-2`
  regressed to **4.1426** and produced a step-10 pre-clip gradient norm of **7.07**. This
  locates the short-run optimum around `1e-3`–`3e-3` rather than merely among sparse points.
- The formal `1e-3` run reached validation loss **2.0179** at step 1600 without instability.
  It was deliberately interrupted after a step-1500 checkpoint so the two missing sweeps
  would not contend for MPS. Because `3e-3`'s 100-step advantage was only 0.0364 and does
  not establish long-horizon safety, the evidence favors resuming rather than discarding the
  stable full-run trajectory.
- The first real MPS resume exposed that `torch.load(map_location="mps")` also moved saved
  CPU RNG byte tensors to MPS, which `Generator.set_state` rejects. Restoring RNG tensors
  explicitly on CPU fixed it. The resumed step-1525 training loss was exactly
  **2.0825698376**, matching the pre-interruption record and demonstrating trajectory-level
  recovery on the real run.
- Completed the handout's Apple Silicon run: batch 32 × 5,000 steps × context 256 =
  **40,960,000 tokens**. Validation loss decreased monotonically from **9.2500** to
  **1.6903**, crossed the relaxed ≤2.00 target at step 1,800, and reached its best observed
  value at the final checkpoint. Trajectory wall time was **3,264 s (54.4 min)**; gradients
  remained around 0.4–0.5 while cosine LR decayed to 1e-4.
- The owner correctly pointed out an orchestration limitation: a detached training process
  does not itself wake the agent. Completion reporting requires keeping a turn active and
  polling, an explicit monitor/automation, or a subsequent user turn. The final segment was

  does not require NaNs. At max LR `1e-1`, training loss spiked to 18.05 and the run settled

  partially recovered to 5.83 after cosine decay reduced LR. The useful short-run region
